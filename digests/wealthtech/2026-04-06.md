[**The Checkout Is Dead: Agentic Payments and the Invisible Commerce Stack**](https://www.fintechbrainfood.com/p/the-checkout-is-dead)
Traditional checkout flows collapse when purchases move into AI agents — conversion tanks and UX breaks — making the case that commerce infrastructure must be rebuilt natively for machines, not humans. The piece maps five levels of agentic commerce, from agent-assisted checkout to fully autonomous anticipatory purchasing, and argues that protocols like MPP (Machine Payments Protocol) are the CLI/API equivalent for agent-native payments. Critical emerging concerns include agent identity (Know Your Agent), audit trails, liability for compromised agents, and the need for entirely new security models.
_Why it matters: The company that solves machine-native payments infrastructure — not better checkout UX — will own the commerce layer of the agentic economy._
→ [SY Taylor](https://www.fintechbrainfood.com/p/the-checkout-is-dead)

[**Signet: Real-Time Kill Switch and Audit Layer for Autonomous Agent Spending**](https://getsignet.xyz/)
Signet monitors every transaction made by every agent across all protocols and payment rails in real time, enforcing pre-defined spending policies and flagging questionable payments for manual review. Its headline capability: revoking any agent's payment access in 500 milliseconds — critical when third-party agents go rogue or are compromised.
_Why it matters: As enterprises deploy autonomous spending agents at scale, a real-time control plane with near-instant revocation isn't a nice-to-have — it's table stakes for risk management._

[**Conto Finance: Policy Enforcement in the Payment Path, Not the Prompt**](https://conto.finance)
Conto Finance controls agent spending by placing policy enforcement directly in the payment execution layer rather than relying on prompt-level instructions, which can be bypassed or hallucinated. The platform maps agent-to-vendor relationships including third-party connections and builds a "trust collective" of known agents to reduce fraud risk.
_Why it matters: Prompt-level guardrails for agentic spending are notoriously fragile — moving enforcement into the payment path itself is the architecturally correct approach._

_Why it matters: Protocol fragmentation is the biggest near-term obstacle to agentic commerce at scale, and a credible aggregation layer could become foundational infrastructure._
→ [SY Taylor](https://www.halofy.ai)
